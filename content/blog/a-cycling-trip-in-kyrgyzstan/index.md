![Bicycle trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-01.jpeg)

Before I’ve been invited to this trip, I knew very little about Kyrgyzstan. So I started reading about this central asian country with lots of mountains and plenty of water and only 6.7 Million people; mostly Muslim but a small percentage are practicing and the state is not influenced much by it.

The trip was planned for 2 weeks and a combination of cycling in the nicer, rural, remote roads but using vans and a support truck (to carry all the bicycles) while trying to reach from a city which is +200Km via narrow roads shared by cars.

[Kyrgyzstan on bicycle; Music around a Yurt near Song Kol Lake #shorts](https://youtube.com/shorts/2BF55XB-F7w?feature=share)

We flew by FlyDubai and payed around 70Euros for each bike, oh… and we had around 20 bikes in our group. FlyDubai was our only choice who guaranteed to accept 20 bicycles. If you know how you should pack a bicycle [for airplane, a quick search will show you.](https://www.google.com/search?q=how+to+pack+bicycle+for+flight&oq=how+to+pack+bicycle+for+flight&client=tablet-android-samsung-nf-rev1&sourceid=chrome-mobile&ie=UTF-8)

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-02.jpeg)

Bishkek is the capital. Mainly 1 or 2 floor houses with friendly people. Less than 2% of Kyrgyzstans GDP comes from tourism and people might start talking to you to practice their English or ask “where are you from”. The population is a mix of Kyrgyz (66%), Uzbek (11%), Russian (11%), … and if you do not know Kyrgyz language, you might have become lucky with some Turkish (Azari) or Russian.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-03.jpeg)

You can spend one or two days with your bike in Bishkek and visit the Container Bazar (which as you can guess, is build by stacking 1000s of containers) and lots of Soviet monuments and enjoy the wide streets which occasional bike lanes.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-04.jpeg)

Our group of ˜20 cyclists needed a support cat and this was it. These soviet era [army?] trucks are one of the few vehicles capable of reaching remote areas where nomad people still live.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-05.jpeg)

To reach places like Sung Kul, you have to pass these nice rivers and single tracks. Its a mountainous country and its very normal to cycle 50–90Km a day with an elevation of ˜1200m. A mountain bike is a good choice because of rocks and muds in some areas but we’ve also seen some cyclists riding hybrids and cyclocrosses.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-06.jpeg)

In some parts we had to carry the bicycles by hand but just imagine that after a long day of cycling uphills, you reach the top of the last hill with a welcoming bench, a place to rest, and an amazing view of the mountains and the traditional nomad tents (called Yurt) which you are going to stay in for the next 2 days only 300 meters from you. We were too tired that day and took a quick rest and some refreshment but returned back the day after to celebrate the view.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-07.jpeg)

These are the Kyrgyzstany traditional tents which we stayed in for 2 days. In July, we had to wear warm and the wood fire heaters was started after 19:00 .

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-08.jpeg)

Yurt’s roof can be opened / closed using the ropes connected to a separated piece but if you open it, you should be quick and close it when the clouds are approaching!

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-09.jpeg)

In this area, we rented horses and you can even spend a day visiting the area by them. Ask before hand for a guide.

And if there are new guests…

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-10.jpeg)

…you can help in raising a new Yurt from scratch. Note how one person mounts the “root” piece while others are trying to mount some poles to it and to the body to keep it in place.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-11.jpeg)

There are Hot Water Springs in the area. For some you have to pay around 200Сом (pronounced Som because they are using the Cyrillic alphabet) but if you ask your host or follow the narrow paths (and become lucky) you might find much cooler places ;)

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-12.jpeg)

![Hot spring tubs in Kyrgyzstan](img-13.jpeg)
*Hot spring tubs in Kyrgyzstan*

After two days of rest, we left this place and continued the mountainous roads downhills toward a cheap but decent resort near another lake.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-14.jpeg)

Kyrgyzstan is one of the last countries int he world where people use eagles for hunting. Nowadays most of these hunting eagles are old and only used for photography by tourists.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-15.jpeg)

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-16.jpeg)

Most of the roads between cities are narrow and not very safe to ride. Fortunately we had a support car in most of the areas and spent the longer between-city roads in a Van.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-17.jpeg)

We spent the last day in Bishkek and visited the cities Aqua Park. Small but a nice place to relax after 11 days of cycling.

![A Cycling Trip in Kyrgyzstan](img-18.jpeg)
